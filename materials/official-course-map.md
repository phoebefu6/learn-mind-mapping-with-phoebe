## The course in one line

Most notes are a list in the order things were said. A mind map is the same content
shaped the way it will be used: one centre, a handful of branches, one keyword each.
Six sessions on making that shape, knowing when it is the wrong shape, and what the
evidence does and does not say about it.

**Running situation:** Daybreak, the coffee subscription company from the SQL, dataviz
and visual-storytelling courses. One planning meeting about the autumn roast launch,
captured fourteen ways.

| Cunningham (2005): 80 students, 80% said mind mapping helped them understand science | B | Wikipedia |
| Holland, Holland & Davies (2004): 62.5% of art and design students vs 34% of computing students agreed mind-mapping software improved understanding | B | Wikipedia |
| 2026 nursing quasi-experiment: 22.07 vs 20.15 mean achievement | C | Search snippet; not quoted on pages |

**How the pages treat this.** Session 1 teaches the disagreement: the effects are real
and consistently positive in headline numbers, the designs are consistently weak, the
one study that measured motivation found it dropped, and the one crossover study that
ran two topics has a results table that disagrees with its own abstract.

**Verification note (2026-09-18).** Every number from the 2026 meta-analysis and from
Jabade 2024 was re-checked by grepping the raw PMC HTML with curl, after a sibling
research run caught a fetch summariser inventing a results table for a PDF. All the
2026 figures reproduced. One Jabade figure ("over 90% agreed") did not exist in the
source and was removed; the abstract-vs-table contradiction was found in the same pass. The honest claim is narrow: making a map is an
active, organising thing to do with material, and active organising beats passive
re-reading. Whether the radial shape adds anything beyond the organising is not
established. No page says "mind maps improve memory by X percent".

### Capacity

| Fact | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Miller (1956), *Psychological Review* 63:81: about seven chunks, plus or minus two, offered by Miller himself as a rough estimate and rhetorical device rather than a measured limit | B | Wikipedia; Britannica; the paper on archive.org (not fetched) |
| Cowan (2001), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 24:87-114: the focus of attention averages **about four chunks** in adults when rehearsal and chunking are prevented | B | PhilPapers record; Semantic Scholar listing |

**How the pages treat this.** Both numbers are taught, neither is called a law about
mind maps. The branch budget the course recommends is "four to seven main branches, and
if you are past seven you are not grouping". The bench verdict uses seven as its upper
bound and says so.

## The bench (`assets/map-live.js`)

One outline in a textarea. The engine parses it into a tree, lays the tree out radially
(each first-level branch gets a slice of the circle proportional to the leaves it
carries; children share the parent's slice evenly), draws it as SVG, then reads nine
numbers back. Learners can paste their own notes from any meeting.

### What is measured

| Metric | How |
|---|---|
| Main branches | Children of the root in the parsed tree |
| Levels deep | Deepest node, centre not counted |
| Words per node, and share that are 1-2 words | Whitespace split of every label |
| Fattest to thinnest branch | Max leaves under one branch divided by min |
| Ideas living in two branches | Labels (normalised) that appear under more than one main branch |
| Labels colliding | Pairs of `rect.mb-lab` boxes that intersect, read with `getBBox()` after painting |
| Labels off the page | Boxes extending past the 880 x 800 viewBox |
| Smallest label | 11.5px times the rendered width over 880, read from `getBoundingClientRect()` |

### What is a heuristic

The one-line verdict. It is labelled "heuristic" on the widget. Rules, in order: no
branches; one branch or a flat list of more than seven; more than seven main branches;
more than two labels colliding or off the page; under 60% short labels; a shared idea;
otherwise good if three to seven branches and nothing collides.

**There is no recall model.** The course does not estimate memory, because the
evidence above does not support a number.

### The five presets

| id | Label | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| `captured` | As captured | Root plus 14 sentences, one level |
| `grouped` | Grouped into branches | 5 branches, the same 14 sentences under them |
| `keywords` | One keyword per branch | 5 branches, 1-2 word nodes, 3 levels; one idea (legal sign-off) deliberately lives under two branches |
| `onehome` | One home per idea | As `keywords`, legal sign-off lives once under Packaging with a "see Comms" pointer |
| `rim` | **ANTI** Every point its own branch | 26 keywords straight off the centre |

### Verified ladder

Derived headlessly (Playwright, 1100px viewport, stage rendered at 904px) on
2026-09-18 via `window.MAP_LIVE`, before any page quoted it. Cumulative in this order.

| Preset | Branches | Deep | Words/node | 1-2 words | Fat/thin | Shared | Colliding | Off page | Verdict (heuristic) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| As captured | 14 | 1 | 11.5 | 0% | 1x | 0 | **9** | 0 | A list wearing a circle |
| Grouped into branches | 5 | 2 | 8.7 | 26% | 2.5x | 0 | 7 | **7** | The labels do not fit the page |
| One keyword per branch | 5 | 3 | 1.7 | 97% | 3.5x | **1** | 0 | 0 | Readable, and one idea lives in two branches |
| One home per idea | 5 | 3 | 1.7 | 97% | 3x | 0 | **0** | **0** | Reads at a glance |
| **ANTI** Every point its own branch | **26** | 1 | 1.8 | 96% | 1x | 0 | **13** | 0 | A list wearing a circle |

Three rows carry the session's hardest lessons:

- **Grouping alone makes it worse on the page.** Five branches with sentences still on
  them: collisions drop from 9 to 7, but 7 labels now run off the page, because a
  sentence at radius 270 is longer than the room it has. The verdict gets worse before
  it gets better, and that is the honest order of operations.
- **Keywords alone are not the fix either.** The anti preset is 96% keywords and it is
  the worst state in the bench: 26 branches, 13 collisions. The keyword rule and the
  branch budget only work together.
- **The last rung is invisible on the page and visible in the count.** `keywords` and
  `onehome` draw almost identically. One number separates them, and it is the one that
  tells you which diagram you are drawing.

"Smallest label" was 11.8px at every rung at this viewport; it moves with the viewport,
not with the preset, which is why it is measured rather than assumed.

### Honest limits

- **Layout is deliberately plain.** A smarter engine could hide collisions the learner
  should see. The rule used is the ordinary radial one.
- **Collision counts depend on label width**, estimated at 6.6px a character. Real
  glyph widths vary; the count is a faithful reading of the drawn boxes, not of
  perfectly measured text.
- **"Ideas living in two branches" matches normalised labels exactly.** Two phrasings
  of one idea will not be caught. The metric is a signal, not a detector.
- **Smallest label changes with the viewport.** That is the point of measuring it; a
  map that reads at 880px may not on a phone.
- **The verdict is a rule of thumb** and says so on the widget.

## Not covered, by design

- Buzan's claims about hemispheric specialisation and "radiant thinking" as a model of
  the brain. The laws are taught as craft rules that make a page readable; the
  neurology attached to them in the books is not endorsed and not attacked.
- Mind-mapping software feature tours (XMind, MindMeister, Miro). Named, not taught.
- Mermaid syntax beyond the two lines needed to show that indentation is the map.
- Sketchnoting: icons, containers, lettering while listening. Next course in the section.
- Memory palaces and mnemonic systems. Adjacent, not this.
