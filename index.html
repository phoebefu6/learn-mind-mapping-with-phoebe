Learn Mind Mapping with Phoebe

Here are the notes from a forty-minute planning meeting: fourteen lines, in the order things were said. Drawn around a centre exactly as written, nine of the fourteen labels land on top of each other. The same fourteen facts sorted into five branches and cut to one keyword each: none collide, and the whole meeting reads at a glance. Six sessions on that shape: what it is, what the research does and does not say about it, when a concept map or a plain list is the honest choice, and a bench that measures your map off the drawn page.

The six sessions 🎯 · one meeting, six shapes

The running situation is Daybreak's autumn roast launch: one planning meeting at the coffee-subscription company from the SQL, dataviz and visual-storytelling courses. Fourteen things were said. Every session gives them a different shape, so the only thing that ever changes is what you did with them.

🌿 Session 1 · easy

One centre, many branches

A list is the order things were said. The four parts of a map, Buzan's ten laws read as layout rules rather than brain claims, and the research quoted with both halves of every abstract.

▶ Start here 🔑 Session 2 · easy

Keywords and the branch budget

Why four and seven are both quoted as the limit and what the argument is really about. One keyword per branch run on real notes, the twig test, and what to do with the ninth branch.

four or seven 🔀 Session 3 · medium

Mind map, concept map, or list

The cross-link test: an idea that belongs to two branches is telling you which diagram you are drawing. Novak's propositions, Eppler's four formats, and when a plain list is the honest choice.

one parent or many 📏 Session 4 · bench night

The map bench

One outline, five shapes, nine numbers read off the drawn map: branches, depth, words per node, balance, shared ideas, collisions, off-page labels. Paste your own notes and watch them move.

★ the bench 📝 Session 5 · medium

Maps as text

Indentation is the map. Why a text outline diffs, versions and survives the tool that made it, when to render it and when to draw by hand, and the two lines of Mermaid you need.

outline first 🧭 Session 6 · hands-on

Maps that do work

Planning, briefing, deciding and revising with a map, and the hand-off: a map is where thinking happens, an outline is where it ships. Final scorecard on a map you actually owe somebody.

What is honest here 🧾

The bench in session 4 reads its numbers off the drawn map. The one-line verdict is a rule of thumb and is labelled as one on the widget. There is no recall model, because the evidence does not support one.

ClaimStatus
Branches, depth, words per node, balance, shared ideasReally counted in the parsed outline you can see and edit
Labels colliding, labels off the page, smallest labelReally measured off the rendered SVG with getBBox() after it is drawn. If the layout fails, the numbers say so
The verdict lineA heuristic, labelled as one on the widget. Its two thresholds, four and seven, are the two capacity estimates session 2 teaches side by side
"Mind maps improve recall by X"Not claimed anywhere. The research is quoted with both halves of every abstract, including the study whose table disagrees with its own summary
